# Room 1 — Junior Security Analyst Intro

## What This Room Covers
An introduction to the Junior SOC Analyst role — what the job actually looks like day-to-day, how the SOC is structured, and where a Tier 1 analyst fits in.

---

## The SOC Tier Structure

A SOC is organized into 3 tiers based on skill level and responsibility:

| Tier | Role | Responsibility |
|------|------|---------------|
| Tier 1 | Junior Analyst | Monitors alerts, triages, escalates |
| Tier 2 | Senior Analyst | Investigates escalated incidents deeper |
| Tier 3 | Expert / Threat Hunter | Proactively hunts threats, handles complex cases |

---

## What a Tier 1 Analyst Does Day-to-Day

- Monitors the SIEM dashboard for incoming alerts
- Triages each alert — determines if it's a real threat or a false positive
- Escalates confirmed threats to Tier 2 with notes and context
- Documents findings and maintains tickets
- Runs 24/7 in shifts — the SOC never sleeps

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| SOC | Security Operations Centre — the team that monitors and responds to security events |
| Triage | The process of evaluating an alert to decide if it's a real threat or not |
| False Positive | An alert that fired but turned out to be harmless |
| Escalation | Passing a confirmed incident to a higher-tier analyst for deeper investigation |
| SIEM | Security Information and Event Management — the tool that aggregates and alerts on security events |

---

## My Takeaway
The Tier 1 role is not just "watching a screen." It requires systematic thinking — every alert needs to be evaluated with a process, not a gut feeling. Triage is a skill, and doing it consistently and correctly is what makes a good L1 analyst.
